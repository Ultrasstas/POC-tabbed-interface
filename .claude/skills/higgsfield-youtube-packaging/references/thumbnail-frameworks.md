# Thumbnail frameworks playbook

Digest of the owner's pinned guide "Thumbnail Frameworks" (Higgsfield AI YouTube channel playbook, 2026,
12 pages). It maps that channel's covers to 16 engaging thumbnail frameworks: 10 in use, 6 not used yet.
Use it as the reference when briefing new covers. The example covers belong to that channel: learn the
mechanism, do not copy their artwork, faces, logos or titles.

**Briefing rule:** when brainstorming a new cover, produce at least 5 options across different
frameworks, and always keep an information gap between the thumbnail and the click.

## Frameworks in use

| # | Framework | How it works | Rules of use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Compelling screenshot + title card | A cinematic still straight from the film, a series-style title card at the bottom, a corner badge (SHORT FILM / FEATURE FILM) signalling the format. | Authenticity is the hook: the frame promises exactly what the viewer gets in the first seconds; the title card creates the "what is this show?" gap. |
| 02 | Posed action shot | A high-energy frame built or exaggerated to capture the single most intriguing action: grimaces, motion blur, explosions, mid-air saves. | One clear subject, one action; supporting text stays short so the action reads at feed size. |
| 03 | Posed portrait | A calm, direct-to-camera portrait signals intimacy and expertise: "I'll walk you through this personally." | For deep-dive tutorials and reviews where trust matters more than hype; simple background so the face carries the frame. |
| 04 | Social UI + proof numbers | Revenue charts, view-count badges and app screenshots act as evidence for the title's money/views claim. Strongest gap for business-facing videos. | Numbers must be honest and traceable to the video, or trust erodes fast. |
| 05 | Adding text (answer / continuation) | Big display text that continues or answers the title: "AI Videos Look Bad? Here's Why" → FIX YOUR AI VIDEOS. | 3–5 words max, one contrasting colour, never repeat the title verbatim. |
| 06 | Before / after transformation | Split-screen or contrast frames showing the jump: bad vs cinema, retro vs today, green screen vs finished shot. | Show the most drastic, instantly readable difference; check-marks and crosses help the split read in under a second. |
| 07 | Product / tool as hero | Logos, app icons and recognisable tool marks are the subject, instantly telling which stack the video is about. | Works best paired with a result promise ($/day) or an unexpected combination. |
| 08 | Amplified reality | A real story element exaggerated for instant communication: a giant photoreal bee, flying debris around the creator. | The exaggeration must still be part of the actual video; push too far and the audience stops trusting the covers. |
| 09 | Episode & format badges | Corner badges (EP. 1, BTS, NEW, SPECIAL EP.) signal a series and invested effort, like "Day #" in challenge content. | Consistent placement (top-right) trains returning viewers to spot new episodes. |
| 10 | Repetition of objects | Multiple characters, panels or products in one frame communicate volume and variety: "there's a lot inside this video." | Keep each repeated object readable at feed size; anchor the frame with one main subject. |

**Stacking:** the best-performing covers combine two or more frameworks, e.g. posed portrait + social UI
proof, or posed action + amplified reality + size difference.

## Not used yet on that channel (cheap A/B tests)

| Framework | Idea |
|---|---|
| Three-step progression | Three panels for a workflow: prompt → raw generation → final graded shot. |
| Highlighting a specific day | "Day 21 of making …" for production diaries. |
| Landscape | An epic environment as the hero with one small subject. |
| Map / aerial | For location or "where it's made" stories. |
| News clip | A news-graphic frame for milestone stories, only if it really made press. |
| Graphical representation | Simple diagrams (org chart, flow) carrying business/agency videos. |

## Recurring visual conventions seen in the examples

- One dominant subject; short heavy sans-serif text in one accent colour (acid yellow-green or white) on a dark or flat background.
- Small brand mark in a corner; format/episode badge top-right; title card bottom-left with a thin accent bar.
- The YouTube title adds the context the image withholds (tool names, "full workflow", the number).

# Higgsfield YouTube Packaging

Create packaging that makes the right viewer curious and accurately promises what the video delivers. Preserve the chosen audience, language, style, topic, and approved facts.

## Start with the actual video

Use the supplied video, approved script, transcript, or scene list. Inspect the video's opening and payoff when accessible; otherwise work from the supplied material without implying you watched it.

Identify:

- The target viewer and the question they want answered.
- The central question, discovery, transformation, or consequence.
- The strongest visual moment that genuinely belongs to that story.
- What the first 30 seconds promise and what the video ultimately delivers.
- Whether the premise is factual, hypothetical, fictional, or a reconstruction.

Infer ordinary creative choices from context. Ask only for missing information that materially changes the result. If no usable video or summary exists, request it.

Use channel references to study positioning and visual technique; create original artwork and titles. Preserve user-owned or licensed brand assets when requested, and avoid implying another creator’s endorsement. Use supplied photos, fonts, and other assets within their applicable permissions; a reference link alone is not permission to reproduce its contents. Do not invent research, performance figures, audience comments, or proven virality formulas.

## Develop three different promises

Return three paired title-and-thumbnail concepts by default. Each should test a different reason to click, rather than the same composition with a different color or synonym.

Possible angles include:

- **Consequence:** show the most compelling outcome and make its cause interesting.
- **Scale or contrast:** make an unfamiliar fact understandable through an accurate comparison.
- **Unanswered question:** reveal enough to establish a puzzle that the video actually resolves.

Choose angles that fit this video; these are options, not mandatory categories. For an original composition vocabulary, read [embedded thumbnail patterns](#thumbnail-patterns). These are reusable creative mechanisms, not copied layouts or a promise of virality.

State each pair's expected payoff in one sentence. Reject promises the story cannot support. If the script never answers the question, change the packaging rather than rewriting the approved video.

## Build the thumbnail

Prefer a moment frozen in action: something is changing, approaching, splitting, or opening. This editorial preference does not require manufacturing danger; a reveal or comparison may better suit a quiet subject.

Specify:

- A 16:9 composition for long-form video with one dominant focal point and a clear silhouette. For an explicitly requested Shorts cover, use 9:16 and check the current upload surface instead of recycling a landscape image.
- Subject, action, setting, camera distance, and the visual relationship that creates curiosity.
- Strong separation through lighting, scale, color, or depth without adding clutter.
- A text-safe area away from critical details and the lower-right duration overlay.
- Exact optional overlay copy: zero to five readable words by default.

Text should add a useful piece of information, not repeat the title. No-text concepts are valid. Treat the word limit as the creator's starting preference, not a YouTube rule.

Judge the concept at small mobile size. Remove tiny diagrams, competing subjects, lengthy labels, and weak contrast. Keep scientific comparisons accurate enough to support their explanation.

For hypothetical disasters or speculative science, preserve that framing in the title and visual treatment. Avoid presenting invented events as breaking news or authentic footage.

## Write the complementary title

Make the subject and curiosity understandable without seeing the thumbnail. Keep spoken, natural English unless another language is requested. Put important information early and remove filler.

Let the title and image do different jobs: the image can show a consequence while the title supplies the question, mechanism, or surprising condition. Do not spend both elements saying exactly the same thing.

Avoid unsupported superlatives, fake urgency, invented numbers, and earnings guarantees. Preserve meaningful uncertainty in scientific or historical claims. Do not predict a specific click-through rate or declare a winner before real viewer results exist.

## Prepare or generate the images

Produce a concrete image-generation brief for each concept. Include the composition, subjects, action, style, lighting, text-safe space, aspect ratio, and essential exclusions. Supply exact overlay wording separately so it can be added cleanly if generated text is unreliable.

If the user has attached a prompting skill that supports the selected image model, use it for model-specific prompt construction. This skill supplies the packaging decisions; a compatible prompting skill translates those decisions into generation instructions. The companion higgsfield-seedance-prompts skill is for video scenes, so do not apply its video-only syntax to thumbnail images. Without a compatible image prompting skill, use a clear self-contained brief and the image model's actual supported controls.

Higgsfield generates the images when image generation is requested. Use [embedded image tool routing](#packaging-tool-routing) for the verified image route and check the live tool schema. A request to create or generate thumbnail images should produce actual image jobs, not stop at written briefs when the integration is available. A request for concepts or prompts alone does not authorize paid generation. Seedance is a video route; do not send a thumbnail to it.

When generation is authorized, generate one image for each distinct concept; three random variations of one prompt do not test three ideas. Inspect each at full size and at a small mobile preview for exact spelling, legible text, focal hierarchy, accurate objects, meaningful contrast, artifacts, and the intended angle. Keep all test images at the same final resolution. Correct a failed image locally within scope; do not restart all three or enter open-ended generation loops.

Label deliverables as **concept**, **generation prompt**, or **generated thumbnail**. Claim completion only with a returned result. If generation is unavailable, deliver usable briefs and state that briefly.

## Complete the upload copy

When a full packaging kit is requested, include:

- **Description:** a clear opening about what viewers will learn, followed by useful context and requested links. Mark missing links as placeholders rather than inventing URLs.
- **Sources:** relevant supplied or genuinely consulted sources for factual claims. Keep fact, interpretation, and speculation distinguishable.
- **Chapters:** only with actual supplied timestamps or timestamps checked against the finished edit. If timing is unavailable, provide chapter names without times or omit chapters; do not turn script estimates into publication timestamps.
- **Tags:** a short, focused set of relevant topic terms and alternate names. Avoid unrelated trending keywords and keyword stuffing.
- **Disclosure note, when relevant:** flag realistic synthetic or altered scenes for the creator to review during upload. If asked for exact disclosure requirements or policy advice, verify the current official YouTube guidance and cite it. Do not invent a universal rule that every AI asset requires the same label or guarantees monetization.

Do not add affiliate language, sponsorship claims, or monetization promises unless supplied and appropriate to the video.

## Deliver a decision-ready kit

Keep the response easy to use:

1. State the video's audience and core promise briefly.
2. Present three named pairs: title, thumbnail scene, exact overlay text or “none,” and the distinct hypothesis each tests.
3. Include the image briefs or actual generated images, according to the requested scope.
4. Provide upload copy if requested.
5. Recommend one starting pair and explain its fit in one or two sentences. Treat this as a creative judgment, not a measured result.

Where an actual A/B test is planned, choose what is being tested: thumbnails only with a fixed title, titles only with a fixed image, or three distinct title-and-thumbnail pairs. Preserve meaningful differences. The official test currently uses watch-time share; a clickbait image that attracts the wrong viewer is not automatically the winner. Read actual reported results and allow an inconclusive outcome. Verify current eligibility and controls before giving setup instructions; Shorts covers and Shorts A/B testing are separate features. Do not upload, start or stop a test unless requested.

End with usable assets, not a generic growth lecture. This skill prepares packaging; it does not upload, publish, change channel settings, or submit tests without separate user authorization.

## Embedded thumbnail patterns

# Original thumbnail composition vocabulary

Select a mechanism because it expresses this video's real question. Do not stack every device into one image. These patterns are creative options, not performance measurements or templates taken from named creators.

| Mechanism | Image construction | What the title contributes | Reject when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before the consequence | Freeze the instant just before a consequential action; show the subject and what it could affect. | The condition or question that makes the moment matter. | The video never depicts or explains that consequence. |
| Familiar scale anchor | Put an unfamiliar object beside one recognizable size reference. | The surprising scale relationship. | Perspective or fictional sizes would imply a false measurement. |
| Hidden interior | Reveal one meaningful cross-section or internal layer with a clear focal point. | The mechanism the viewer will understand. | The internal design is invented but presented as a measured fact. |
| One variable changed | Show the same place or object under two conditions, with an obvious visual difference. | The single change that causes the result. | Several unrelated differences make the comparison misleading. |
| Unexpected neighbor | Place two scientifically related objects together where the connection is not immediately obvious. | The reason they belong together. | The connection exists only to manufacture a mystery. |
| Missing essential part | Remove one recognizable component and make the resulting consequence visible. | What happens when that part is absent. | The omission makes the subject unrecognizable. |
| Visible threshold | Put a clear boundary, level, or break point in the scene. | Why crossing it changes the outcome. | The number or threshold is unsupported. |
| Path toward a destination | Use one trajectory, route, or narrowing corridor to direct the eye. | Where it leads and what the video investigates. | Multiple arrows and tiny annotations compete with the subject. |
| Time compressed into a scene | Contrast two stages through a controlled split or a readable progression. | The meaningful period or process. | The time claim is invented or the stages cannot be read at small size. |
| Macro becoming a world | Enlarge one small structure until its shape becomes surprising, with a scale cue when useful. | What it is and why the closer view matters. | A fantasy texture is passed off as authentic microscopy. |
| One anomaly in a pattern | Use a repeated field with one deliberate, relevant exception. | What makes the exception important. | The exception is not explained in the video. |
| A calm place under a strange condition | Establish one familiar scene and one visually impossible but clearly hypothetical rule. | The What If premise and the question being answered. | The packaging resembles real breaking news about a fabricated event. |

## Turn a pattern into a generation brief

Specify the exact subject, action or state, camera distance, foreground/background relationship, palette, light, one curiosity cue, text-safe area and exact optional overlay. Say what the viewer must understand at a glance. Keep the overlay at zero to five words as an editorial starting point; a clear image may need none.

Create three materially different briefs for a three-option kit. For example, one can emphasize the consequence, another the mechanism, and another an accurate scale comparison. Do not call arbitrary color changes three separate hypotheses.

## Evaluate before delivery

- Can a new viewer identify the subject at a small mobile preview?
- Is there one dominant question rather than several unrelated hooks?
- Does the image promise something the opening actually begins to deliver?
- Does the title add information instead of restating the same words?
- Are text, objects and numbers accurate and readable?
- Does the image remain clear without a logo, arrow, emoji or dramatic face added by habit?

If one answer fails, fix that specific design problem. A visual score is an editorial judgment; do not invent a predicted CTR, retention percentage or guaranteed viral label.

## Embedded image tool routing

# Generate thumbnail images through Higgsfield

The adapter below was checked against the connected catalog on 2026-11-10. Read the actual available tool schema before submitting. Tool namespaces differ by host; the short names describe the observed functions, not permission to invent endpoints.

## Route

- Use `generate_image` for thumbnail images. Its current default model is `gpt_image_2_6`. Preserve a different image model explicitly selected by the user and inspect its supported parameters when needed.
- Put generation inputs inside `params`: `model`, `prompt`, `aspect_ratio`, and supported model-specific controls. Use `count: 1` for each distinct concept. Make three concept-specific calls or use an available batch endpoint with three different prompts and the required preflight flow.
- Current GPT Image 2.6 supports `16:9`, `9:16`, PNG/JPEG/WebP, and `1k`, `2k`, `4k` resolution tiers. It also exposes a `size` field. For a long-form publication-ready default, request 16:9, 4k, 3840×2160 where supported; for a Shorts cover, request 9:16, 2160×3840. Inspect the actual output dimensions. A resolution string in prompt prose does not set the exported file size.
- Follow `estimate_image_cost` and provider credit-choice requirements when applicable. Reuse this conversation's authorized project destination. If none exists, use `get_preferences` before the first generation when the connector requires it; do not create an unsolicited project.
- Use only confirmed references and the supported `medias` mechanism. A generic image reference has canonical `role: "image"`. A supplied screenshot used for analysis is not automatically a license to copy its artwork, face, characters or analytics.

Illustrative long-form input shape:

```json
{
  "params": {
    "model": "gpt_image_2_6",
    "prompt": "The complete original thumbnail concept goes here, including exact optional text.",
    "aspect_ratio": "16:9",
    "resolution": "4k",
    "size": "3840x2160",
    "quality": "high",
    "output_format": "png",
    "count": 1
  }
}
```

Replace the illustrative prompt with the concept. Choose cost and quality within the user's request and available controls. Do not call the example a completed generation.

## Finish the actual deliverable

1. Preserve returned job IDs. Wait with the documented status tool rather than resubmitting after a timeout. The current `jobs_wait` supports at most eight job IDs and 15 seconds per poll.
2. Follow the generation tool's display instructions. The ordinary generation call already creates an updating widget; do not open duplicate widgets merely to refresh it.
3. Inspect spelling, subject details and readability. If generated typography fails, use an authorized image-edit or compositing route to correct it; preserve the working image rather than asking the user to fix a defective deliverable.
4. Export actual PNG/JPEG images at the final ratio and consistent dimensions. Check current upload limits for the user's surface. As checked in November 2026, YouTube recommends 3840×2160 long-form thumbnails; desktop accepts larger files than mobile. Do not treat an old universal 2 MB limit as current for every upload.
5. Give each image its matching title and hypothesis. Do not substitute an unrelated previous generation for a requested variant.

Public tool instructions cannot establish that a thumbnail will go viral. Explain the intended curiosity mechanism and test it with real viewers when that action is requested.

## Current testing distinction

As checked in November 2026, YouTube's desktop A/B feature can compare up to three titles, thumbnails, or paired combinations for eligible videos, using watch-time share. It does not support Shorts testing. Desktop custom Shorts thumbnails are a different upload feature. Recheck official guidance before advising on a later UI or eligibility rule; do not turn a stored snapshot into a permanent promise.
